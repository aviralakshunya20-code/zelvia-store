Why Calibrating Your Screen Is Essential

Web browsers natively operate in CSS pixels, not real-world units. Because physical screens have vastly differing pixel densities (DPI/PPI)—ranging from around 100 PPI on desktop displays to over 450 PPI on modern mobile screens—a generic CSS centimeter cannot guarantee physical accuracy.

Naapu solves this by using the standardized ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1 standard. Any bank card, driver's license, or Aadhaar PVC card in your wallet has an identical length of exactly 85.60 mm (3.37 inches). By matching an outline on your screen to a card, the application computes the exact pixels-per-millimeter ratio (pxPerMm) for your specific hardware.

The 8 Themed Measurement Worlds

Naapu divides real-world dimension learning into 8 progressive categories:

  • World 1: Jeb ke Sikke (Pocket Coins) – Common currency coins (₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10) and standard credit cards.
  • World 2: Battery Bazaar (Batteries & Small Objects) – CR2032 coin batteries, AAA and AA alkaline cells, 9V blocks, and standard playing cards.
  • World 3: Gadget Dabba (Desk Tech) – Nano SIM cards, USB Type-A plugs, 3x3 Rubik's cubes, and optical discs.
  • World 4: Kaagaz Mela (Paper Sizes) – ISO 216 paper dimensions from A6 postcards to large A3 sketch sheets.
  • World 5: Khel ke Gole (Sports Balls) – Standard golf, table tennis, tennis, cricket, and association football balls.
  • World 6: Note Ginti (Currency Notes) – Subtle length differences among modern Indian banknotes (₹10, ₹50, ₹100, ₹500) and US one-dollar bills.
  • World 7: Bade Naap (Large Equipment) – Tennis rackets, cricket stumps, maximum legal cricket bats, and doorway clearances.
  • World 8: Bahut Bade (Courts & Pitches) – Basketball rims, football goalposts, badminton courts, cricket pitches, and regulation tennis courts.
