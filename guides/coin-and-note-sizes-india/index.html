Exact Dimensions of Indian Coins and Currency Notes

Published: October 2026 · Reading Time: 5 mins · Topic: Indian Currency Specifications

The coins and banknotes in your wallet are precision-engineered metallic and cotton artifacts minted under Reserve Bank of India (RBI) standards. Because their official blueprints are tightly controlled, coins and bills make convenient physical reference gauges.

The Sizing Order of Indian Coins

A frequent misconception is that coin diameter grows with monetary value. In circulating Indian coinage, this is not strictly true:

  • The standard ₹1 coin has a diameter of 22.0 mm (the smallest diameter in this four-coin set).
  • The standard ₹2 coin has a diameter of 25.0 mm.
  • The standard ₹5 coin has a diameter of 23.0 mm—smaller in diameter than the ₹2 coin, but noticeably thicker and heavier.
  • The bimetallic ₹10 coin is the largest common circulating coin at 27.0 mm diameter.

The Mahatma Gandhi (New) Series Banknotes

Introduced progressively from late 2016 onward, the modern Indian banknote series features uniform height across several denominations (66 mm height) while systematically incrementing in horizontal length as denomination increases:

  • ₹10 Note (Chocolate Brown): Measures exactly 123.0 mm long.
  • ₹50 Note (Fluorescent Blue): Measures exactly 135.0 mm long (12 mm longer than the ₹10 note).
  • ₹100 Note (Lavender): Measures exactly 142.0 mm long (7 mm longer than the ₹50 note).
  • ₹500 Note (Stone Grey): Measures exactly 150.0 mm long (8 mm longer than the ₹100 note).
  • For international comparison, the standard US One Dollar Bill measures 156.0 mm long, making it slightly longer than any modern circulating Indian rupee note.

Circulation Tolerances & Approximate Variance

While official mint blueprints specify integer millimeters, physical currency in active circulation exhibits small deviations:

  • Coin Rim Wear: Years of mechanical friction and pocket handling smooth out the raised milled rim, occasionally reducing measured diameter by 0.1 mm to 0.3 mm.
  • Older Coin Series: India has issued multiple series of ₹1, ₹2, and ₹5 coins over past decades (such as cupro-nickel cupped issues versus newer ferritic stainless steel). The numbers above reflect current circulating ferritic steel and bimetallic designs. Older commemorative issues may vary; check before relying on older coins for precision work.
  • Paper Note Creasing: Severe folding and humidity can shrink or ruffle paper banknote edges by 1 mm to 2 mm. For maximum calibration fidelity, always prioritize rigid PVC bank cards over paper currency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use an Indian coin to calibrate the screen ruler?

We strongly recommend using an ATM or credit card (85.6 mm) rather than a coin. Because a payment card is longer, sub-pixel rounding errors are diluted over a much larger span, resulting in greater calibration accuracy.

Why is the ₹2 coin wider than the ₹5 coin?

The ₹5 coin is minted noticeably thicker and heavier to maintain tactile distinctiveness for visually impaired citizens while keeping raw material cost balanced.

How can I verify my coin on my screen?

You can calibrate your device and place your coin against the screen in the Online Screen Ruler to measure its diameter down to the millimeter.
