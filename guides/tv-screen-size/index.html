TV and Screen Sizes: Real Width and Height in cm

Updated: November 2026 · Reading time: 5 mins

A "55-inch TV" is not 55 inches wide. Screen sizes are measured diagonally, from one corner of the visible picture to the opposite corner, and they leave out the frame. To know whether a TV fits a wall or a cabinet, you need its real width and height, which depend on the diagonal and the shape (aspect ratio) of the screen.

How the Maths Works

For a screen with aspect ratio a:b and diagonal d, Pythagoras gives width = d × a ÷ √(a² + b²) and height = d × b ÷ √(a² + b²). For 16:9 that means the width is about 87% of the diagonal and the height about 49%. One inch is exactly 2.54 cm.

Choosing a TV Size for Your Room

A common rule for HD and 4K TVs is to sit about 1 to 1.5 times the screen's diagonal away. For a 55-inch TV that is roughly 1.4–2.1 m. Closer than that and you will notice pixels or turn your head; further and the picture feels small. Measure the wall or cabinet first, then use the table to check the full width including the frame.

Frequently Asked Questions

How wide is a 55 inch TV in cm?

The picture of a 16:9 55-inch TV is about 121.8 cm wide and 68.5 cm high. With the frame, most 55-inch TVs are about 123–125 cm wide.

Is screen size measured with the frame?

No. Screen size is the diagonal of the visible picture only. The frame (bezel) adds width and height on top of the numbers in this guide.

How many cm is a 32 inch TV?

The diagonal is 81 cm. The picture is about 70.8 cm wide and 39.8 cm high.

What size TV should I buy for my room?

Sit about 1 to 1.5 times the diagonal away from a 4K TV. For a viewing distance of 2 metres, a 55- to 65-inch screen usually feels right.
