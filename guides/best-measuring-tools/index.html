Our calibrated screen ruler is great for anything that fits on your screen: a screw, a coin, a ring, a business card. But a phone screen is only about 15 cm long. To measure a sofa, a window, a wrist or a 3D-printed part to a tenth of a millimetre, you need a physical tool.

1. A 25 ft (7.5 m) tape measure

Best for: furniture, doors, windows, everyday DIY

If you only own one measuring tool, make it this. Look for a blade marked in both inches and centimetres, a wide blade (about 1 inch / 25 mm) that stays straight when extended, a lock button, and a belt clip. A true-zero hook that slides slightly lets you measure accurately both inside and outside an edge.

2. A laser distance measure

Best for: rooms, ceilings, floor plans, measuring alone

Point, press, read. A laser measure gives you wall-to-wall distances in a second without a helper, and most compact models reach 30–50 m. Useful extras are area and volume modes (handy for paint and flooring) and a backlit screen. Bosch, DeWalt and many smaller brands make good entry-level models.

See laser measures on Amazon

3. A 6-inch digital caliper

Best for: screws, bolts, jewelry, coins, 3D printing

A caliper measures outside size, inside size and depth, usually to 0.01 mm. A 150 mm / 6-inch stainless-steel model covers almost every small object. Look for a large display, an mm/inch toggle button and a zero button so you can measure differences. Avoid all-plastic calipers if you want them to stay accurate.

See digital calipers on Amazon

4. A steel ruler with metric and inch markings

Best for: drawing straight lines, checking paper sizes, crafts

A flat 12-inch / 30 cm stainless-steel ruler is more accurate than a plastic school ruler, doesn't warp, and doubles as a cutting edge. A cork back stops it sliding on paper. It's also the easiest way to double-check your screen ruler's calibration.

5. A soft sewing tape

Best for: body measurements, clothing sizes, curved objects

For a waist, chest, wrist or the outside of a round object, a rigid tape is useless. A 60-inch / 150 cm fibreglass sewing tape bends around curves without stretching. Some come in a retractable case, which keeps them from tangling in a drawer.

6. A ring sizer

Best for: buying a ring online, surprise gifts

Our ring size finder works well if you already have a ring that fits. If you don't, a reusable plastic ring sizer (a strip or a set of loops) measures the finger itself. Jewelers use a metal finger-gauge set and a ring mandrel for the most accurate result.

See ring sizers on Amazon

7. A measuring wheel

Best for: gardens, fences, driveways, sports fields

For distances longer than a tape measure, walk a measuring wheel along the line and read the counter. Larger wheels (about 12 inches / 30 cm) roll more smoothly over grass and gravel. Most fold down for storage.

Tips for getting accurate measurements

  • Measure twice. Write the number down straight away, then measure again before you cut or buy.
  • Measure the inside opening, not the outside frame, when checking if something will fit. Our fit guide explains how much margin to leave.
  • Keep the tape straight and level. A sagging tape over a long distance always reads too long.
  • Check your units. Many tapes and calipers switch between inches and mm. Use our inches to cm chart if you need to convert.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a screen ruler as accurate as a real ruler?

After calibrating with a bank card, an on-screen ruler is accurate to about a millimetre for items that fit on your screen. For anything longer than your screen, or when you need tenths of a millimetre, use a physical tape measure or caliper.

Laser measure or tape measure for measuring a room?

A laser measure is faster for wall-to-wall distances and ceilings, and you can use it alone. A tape measure is better for short lengths, inside cabinets and anything you need to measure around.

What is the most accurate tool for measuring small objects?

A digital caliper. Most read to 0.01 mm (0.0005 in) and measure outside size, inside size and depth, which makes them ideal for screws, jewelry, coins and 3D-printed parts.
