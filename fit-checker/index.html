How the 2D Fit Calculator Works

The Fit Checker evaluates whether an item (with given width and height) can be placed entirely inside an outer container (such as a parcel box, drawer, or luggage compartment) while honoring a designated border clearance margin.

Direct Orientation vs Rotated Fit

An object can fit in either its original orientation or rotated by 90 degrees:

  • Direct Fit: (item.width + 2 × margin ≤ box.width) AND (item.height + 2 × margin ≤ box.height).
  • Rotated 90° Fit: (item.height + 2 × margin ≤ box.width) AND (item.width + 2 × margin ≤ box.height).

Why Safety Margin Matters

In the real world, rigid objects cannot slide into containers that match their exact theoretical dimensions. Extra clearance margin is required to account for:

  • Protective Padding: Bubble wrap, corrugated cardboard, or protective foam (typically 10 mm to 25 mm).
  • Finger Clearance: Space to grasp and lift the item out of a deep drawer or box without tilting.
  • Manufacturing Variance: Slight bends, seams, or corner radii in cardboard and plastic bins.

How the Fit Checker Works

Enter the length and width of the item and of the space (a box, drawer, envelope or bag pocket). The checker compares them with a clearance margin and also tries the item rotated by 90°, because something that doesn't fit lengthways often fits sideways.

Measuring the Space Correctly

  • Measure the inside of boxes and drawers, not the outside: walls can take 3–10 mm.
  • Measure at the narrowest point — drawer runners, hinges and handles often stick out.
  • For bags, measure flat and empty; soft bags lose space when packed.
  • Airline cabin limits include wheels and handles. A common limit in India is 55 × 35 × 25 cm, but always check your airline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the fit checker work in 3D?

It compares two dimensions at a time. For boxes, check length × width first, then run it again with the height.

What does the rotation check do?

It tests the item turned by 90°. Many items that seem too long fit once rotated, especially in square-ish spaces.

How do I measure an item without a tape?

Use the on-screen ruler for small items, or compare with objects of known size such as an A4 sheet (21 × 29.7 cm) or a bank card (8.56 × 5.4 cm).
